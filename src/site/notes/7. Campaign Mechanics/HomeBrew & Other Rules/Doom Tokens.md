## 1. What Is a Doom Token?

A **Doom Token** is a consequence imposed by the DM when a player:

- Plays clearly outside of their established character.
    
- Continues disruptive behaviour after receiving a warning.
    
- Fails to respond to the weekly attendance poll by the deadline.
    

When the DM spends a Doom Token, the player’s declared action or roll becomes an **automatic failure**.

Doom Tokens belong to the **player**, not their current character. Character death, resurrection or replacement does not remove them.

---

## 2. Receiving Doom Tokens

### Playing Outside of Character

The DM may immediately issue **one Doom Token** when a player knowingly acts outside their established character without reasonable narrative justification.

This may include:

- Using information their character could not know.
    
- Ignoring established motivations, values or relationships purely for mechanical benefit.
    
- Abandoning their character’s established behaviour solely for a joke.
    
- Acting against the party without an in-character reason.
    
- Using information obtained during another character’s private scene.
    
- Deliberately undermining a serious roleplay moment.
    
- Using “it is what my character would do” to justify behaviour that contradicts how the character has been established.
    

A warning is **not required** when the out-of-character behaviour is clear.

The player may briefly explain their character’s reasoning. Character development and unexpected decisions are permitted where a reasonable in-character explanation exists. ***Players have 45 seconds to make their point to the DM

The DM makes the final ruling. Any extended discussion must wait until after the session.

---

### Disruptive Behaviour

Disruptive behaviour may include:

- Repeatedly interrupting the DM or another player.
    
- Speaking over another player’s scene.
    
- Shouting over the table.
    
- Repeatedly starting unrelated conversations.
    
- Excessive phone use or inattention.
    
- Requiring information to be repeated because the player was not paying attention.
    
- Continuing a rules argument after the DM has made a ruling.
    
- Deliberately derailing a scene or encounter.
    
- Preventing other players from participating.
    
- Unnecessarily delaying the game.
    

The following process applies:

1. The DM gives the player a clear warning.
    
2. The warning remains active for the rest of that session.
    
3. Any further disruptive incident during that session may earn **one Doom Token**.
    
4. No additional warning is required before further tokens are issued during that session.
    

A single continuous disruption normally counts as one incident. If the player stops and later begins disrupting the game again, it may count as a new incident.

---

### Attendance Polls

Every player must respond to the weekly attendance poll by **11:59 pm on Wednesday**.

- Attending, not attending and uncertain are all valid responses.
    
- A player who does not vote or contact the DM before the deadline automatically receives **one Doom Token**.
    
- No warning or reminder is required.
    
- The token is issued whether or not the player attends that week.
    
- The penalty is for failing to communicate, not for being unavailable.
    

The DM may waive the penalty where a genuine emergency or exceptional circumstance prevented the player from responding.

---

## 3. Doom Token Limit

- Each player may hold a maximum of **five Doom Tokens**.
    
- Doom Tokens stack and carry over between sessions.
    
- Doom Tokens do not expire naturally.
    
- Doom Tokens cannot be transferred between players.
    
- Only the DM may decide when a Doom Token is spent.
    
- Only one Doom Token may be spent on a single action or roll.
    
- Once a player reaches five tokens, no additional tokens are added until at least one has been spent or removed.
    

Doom Tokens must be tracked openly so that every player knows how many they currently hold. Tracking can be found on the [[AAA. Player Homepage#Doom Token Tracker\|AAA. Player Homepage#Doom Token Tracker]]

## 4. Spending a Doom Token

The DM may spend one of a player’s Doom Tokens to make any action or roll performed by that player an **automatic failure**.

The DM must declare the Doom Token:

- After the player declares their intended action; and
    
- Before the outcome is revealed.
    

The DM may declare the Doom Token before or after dice are rolled, provided the final outcome has not yet been announced.

Once declared:

1. One Doom Token is removed from the player.
    
2. The associated action or roll automatically fails.
    
3. Any dice already rolled are disregarded.
    
4. The normal consequences of failure are applied.
    

A natural 20 does not overcome a Doom Token.

Advantage, disadvantage, bonuses, rerolls, reactions and other ordinary abilities cannot prevent the automatic failure.

---

## 5. All Rolls Are Eligible

**Every roll is eligible to be affected by a Doom Token. There are no protected rolls.**


### Rolls Without a Normal Failure State

When a roll does not ordinarily have a success-or-failure result, Doom produces the **worst legal result** for that roll.

Examples include:

- **Damage roll:** The attack or effect deals the minimum possible damage.
    
- **Healing roll:** The target receives the minimum possible healing.
    
- **Hit Die:** The character recovers the minimum possible hit points.
    
- **Initiative:** The character is placed last in the initiative order.
    
- **Random table:** The DM selects the worst applicable legal outcome.
    
- **Recharge or recovery roll:** The resource does not recharge or recover.
    
- **Injury roll:** The least favourable applicable result occurs.
    

The DM determines how automatic failure applies when a roll has an unusual structure.

---

## 6. Consequences of Automatic Failure

A Doom Token creates a genuine failure.

This means:

- An attack misses.
    
- An ability check fails to accomplish its declared objective.
    
- A saving throw is failed.
    
- A concentration check ends concentration.
    
- A contested check is lost.
    
- A death saving throw counts as **one failed death saving throw**.
    
- A roll cannot be rescued by adding a bonus after Doom is declared.
    
- Abilities that convert a failed roll into a success cannot override Doom.
    
- The DM may spend Doom where failure carries severe or permanent consequences.
    

A Doom Token does not automatically create a critical failure unless the relevant rule would ordinarily treat the automatic failure that way.

---

## 7. Interaction With Inspiration Tokens

Inspiration and Doom Tokens are opposing forces.

### Inspiration Declared First

When an Inspiration Token is declared before Doom:

- The DM cannot apply a Doom Token to that action.
    
- The Inspiration Token produces its normal automatic success.
    

### Doom Declared First

When the DM declares a Doom Token, the affected player or another Inspiration Token holder may immediately spend an Inspiration Token to oppose it.

When this happens:

- The Doom Token is spent.
    
- The Inspiration Token is spent.
    
- The automatic failure and automatic success cancel each other.
    
- The original action or roll is resolved normally.
    
- Neither token may be replaced or reapplied to that action.
    

The Inspiration Token holder must declare the cancellation before the Doom failure is resolved.

---

## 8. Removing Doom Tokens

Doom Tokens are normally removed only when:

- The DM spends the token to cause an automatic failure.
    
- An Inspiration Token cancels it.
    
- The DM determines that it was issued because of an error or misunderstanding.
    
- The DM removes it because of an exceptional circumstance.
    

Doom Tokens are not removed through:

- Character death or resurrection.
    
- Changing characters.
    
- Resting.
    
- Levelling up.
    
- Downtime.
    
- Missing a session.
    
- Voluntarily failing an action.
    
- Accepting disadvantage.
    
- Performing well in a later session.
    

Good play may earn an Inspiration Token, but it does not automatically remove existing Doom Tokens.

---

## 9. Examples

### Out-of-Character Play

A character has consistently risked their life to protect innocent people. The player suddenly attacks an innocent NPC solely to obtain an item and cannot provide an in-character explanation.

The DM immediately issues one Doom Token.

### Disruptive Behaviour

A player repeatedly speaks over another player’s scene. The DM gives them a warning.

The player later begins shouting over a different scene. The DM issues one Doom Token without another warning.

### Missed Attendance Poll

The attendance poll closes at 11:59 pm on Wednesday. A player has not voted or contacted the DM.

The player automatically receives one Doom Token.

### Automatic Attack Failure

A player rolls a natural 20 on an attack. Before the outcome is announced, the DM spends one of that player’s Doom Tokens.

The natural 20 is disregarded and the attack automatically misses.

### Automatic Death-Save Failure

A player is making a death saving throw. The DM spends a Doom Token before the outcome is revealed.

The character records one failed death saving throw.

### Doom Against Inspiration

The DM declares Doom against a player’s saving throw. Another player immediately spends their Inspiration Token to intervene.

Both tokens are spent. The automatic success and automatic failure cancel each other, and the saving throw is rolled normally.

One deliberate distinction remains: **automatic failure is not automatically a natural 1 or critical failure**. Doom guarantees failure, but it does not create extra consequences unless another rule specifically makes those consequences part of failing.
