## 1. DM Veto and On-the-Spot Rulings

When a situation is not clearly covered by the official rules or established homebrew rules, the DM has the authority to:

- Make an immediate ruling;
    
- Allow or deny the proposed action;
    
- Determine how an unclear interaction resolves; or
    
- Veto an action, interpretation or outcome entirely.
    

The DM is not required to pause the session to search for a rule that does not have an immediately clear answer.

The ruling applies immediately and play continues unless an affected player uses their right of appeal.

---

## 2. Who May Appeal?

Only a player who is **directly affected by the ruling** may appeal it.

A player is directly affected when the ruling changes:

- Their character’s declared action;
    
- A roll made by or against their character;
    
- Damage, healing or a condition affecting their character;
    
- The use of their character’s spell, item, feature or resource;
    
- Their character’s immediate position or circumstances; or
    
- An outcome that directly concerns their character.
    

Players may not appeal solely because they disagree with a ruling affecting another player.

The affected player may ask another player to quickly provide a relevant rule reference, but this does not extend the appeal period.

---

## 3. The 45-Second Appeal

An affected player has a maximum of **45 seconds** to make their case.

The appeal must clearly explain:

1. What part of the ruling they believe is incorrect;
    
2. The rule, feature or established precedent supporting their position; and
    
3. What outcome they believe should apply instead.
    

The 45 seconds begins when the DM acknowledges the appeal.

During the appeal:

- The affected player has the floor.
    
- Other players must not interrupt or turn the appeal into a group debate.
    
- The DM may ask a brief clarifying question.
    
- The timer continues while books, websites or character sheets are searched.
    
- Failing to locate the relevant wording within the 45 seconds does not delay play.
    

Once the 45 seconds expires, the DM makes a final ruling and the game immediately continues.

---

## 4. Appeal Deadline

A player must appeal a suspected bad ruling **before the beginning of their next turn**.

For a ruling made during the affected player’s turn, the appeal must be raised before that turn ends.

For a ruling made outside the affected player’s turn, the appeal must be raised before their next turn begins.

Outside initiative, the appeal must be raised immediately after the ruling and before the scene meaningfully progresses.

Once the deadline passes:

- The right to appeal that ruling is forfeited;
    
- The completed outcome is not reversed;
    
- Resources spent and consequences suffered remain in place; and
    
- Play will not be rewound.
    

> Not noticing the ruling, becoming distracted or waiting to see whether the outcome is favourable does not extend the appeal deadline.

---

## 5. Final Ruling

After hearing the appeal, the DM may:

- Uphold the original ruling;
    
- Amend the ruling;
    
- Reverse the ruling; or
    
- Make a temporary ruling and review the underlying rule after the session.
    

Once the DM gives their final decision:

- The ruling is final for the current session;
    
- No second appeal is permitted;
    
- Other players may not reopen the argument;
    
- Play resumes immediately; and
    
- Continued debate must wait until after the session.
    

If later information shows that the ruling was incorrect, the DM may correct how the rule operates going forward. The DM is not required to undo completed turns, encounters or scenes.

---

## 6. Straight Veto

The DM may issue a **Straight Veto** without allowing an in-session appeal where the proposed action:

- Is impossible within the established world or current circumstances;
    
- Relies on information the character does not possess;
    
- Contradicts an established campaign rule;
    
- Attempts to exploit ambiguous wording or an obvious rules loophole;
    
- Would seriously disrupt the session;
    
- Violates an agreed table boundary;
    
- Requires content the DM will not include in the campaign; or
    
- Cannot reasonably be resolved without substantially delaying play.
    

A Straight Veto means:

- The proposed action does not occur;
    
- No roll is made;
    
- No character resource is spent unless it was already committed before the veto became necessary;
    
- The decision cannot be appealed during the session; and
    
- Any discussion must take place after the session.
    

The DM should identify the decision as a **Straight Veto** so that players know the ordinary appeal process does not apply.

---

## 7. Misuse of Appeals

The appeal process exists to correct genuine misunderstandings, not to repeatedly pressure the DM into changing an unfavourable result.

The following may be treated as disruptive behaviour:

- Appealing only after seeing that a ruling produced a bad result;
    
- Repeating an argument after the final decision;
    
- Having several players argue the same appeal;
    
- Interrupting another player’s turn to reopen an expired appeal;
    
- Deliberately dragging out the 45-second window;
    
- Presenting the same rejected interpretation repeatedly; or
    
- Using an appeal to dispute narrative consequences rather than a genuine ruling.
    

Where this behaviour continues after a warning, the existing **Doom Token Rules** may apply.

---

## Rule Summary

> When no clear rule exists, the DM makes the ruling and may veto the action. A directly affected player may appeal once, has no more than 45 seconds to make their case, and must raise the appeal before their next turn. After the DM’s final decision, the ruling stands and play continues.
