## 1. Loot Categories

All discovered loot belongs to one of two categories:

1. **Designated Loot**
    
2. **Contested Loot**
    

The DM determines the category and announces it when the loot is discovered. The DM’s classification is final.

---

## 2. Designated Loot

Designated Loot is an item the DM has specifically created, selected or placed for a particular player character.

When Designated Loot is discovered by a player other than the designated:

- The DM announces that the item is designated.
    
- The DM identifies the character for whom the item is intended.
    
- The item is immediately awarded to that character.
    
- No loot roll takes place.
    
- Other players cannot contest, intercept, steal or claim the item during its discovery.
    
- Position, initiative, character speed and proximity do not override the designation.
    
- The designated character may later trade, give away, sell or otherwise dispose of the item voluntarily.
    

The item belongs to the intended character once it has been awarded, but the DM does not control what that character chooses to do with it afterwards.

### Unavailable Designated Character

If the intended character is temporarily absent, unconscious, incapacitated or elsewhere in the immediate scene:

- The item remains reserved for that character.
    
- Another party member may physically carry it when necessary.
    
- Carrying the item does not grant ownership or ability to utilise.
    
- The item must be handed to the designated character at the next reasonable opportunity.
    

If the intended character has permanently left the campaign, the DM may redesignate the item or convert it into Contested Loot.

> **Designated Loot exists to ensure that character-specific rewards reach the character for whom they were created. It is not available for competitive looting.**

---

## 3. Contested Loot

Any loot that the DM does not identify as Designated Loot is considered **Contested Loot**.

When Contested Loot is discovered:

1. The DM determines which characters are in the immediate vicinity.
    
2. The DM announces that the loot is open for rolling.
    
3. Eligible players have **10 seconds** to clearly declare that they wish to roll.
    
4. Every eligible player who declared in time rolls one straight d20.
    
5. The highest result wins the item.
    

The winner gains control and ownership of the item and decides what happens to it.

---

## 4. Immediate Vicinity

A character is in the **immediate vicinity** when, at the exact moment the loot is announced, they meet all of the following conditions:

- They are within **30 feet** of the loot.
    
- There is an unobstructed physical route between the character and the loot.
    
- They are physically present in the same active scene.
    

Distance is measured using the battle map whenever one is available.

An unobstructed route cannot pass through:

- A closed or locked door.
    
- A solid wall.
    
- A sealed container that has not yet been opened.
    
- An impassable barrier.
    
- A separate floor or level without an immediately accessible connection.
    

Characters are not in the immediate vicinity when they are:

- More than 30 feet from the loot.
    
- In another room behind a closed door.
    
- On another floor without being within 30 feet by an accessible route.
    
- Outside the building or defined encounter area.
    
- Participating remotely through a familiar, summon, magical sensor or similar effect.
    
- Arriving after the DM has announced eligibility.
    

Being unconscious, restrained, prone, engaged in combat or otherwise occupied does not affect eligibility, provided the character satisfies the distance, route and scene requirements.

### Theatre of the Mind

When exact distances are not being tracked, the DM determines which characters would reasonably be within 30 feet.

The DM must announce the eligible characters **before starting the 10-second declaration window**. Once announced, that eligibility decision is final for that item.

Players may request clarification before the timer begins, but the distance and eligibility ruling cannot be debated after the timer starts.

---

## 5. The 10-Second Declaration Window

After confirming eligibility, the DM announces something to the effect of:

> “Contested loot. You have 10 seconds to call your roll.”

During those 10 seconds:

- An eligible player must clearly say that they are rolling.
    
- Silence does not count as participation.
    
- Vague reactions, jokes or statements such as “maybe” do not count.
    
- Another player cannot enter someone else into the roll.
    
- Players who declare after the 10 seconds have expired cannot participate.
    
- The timer does not restart because someone was distracted or speaking.
    

The DM may count aloud or use a timer. The DM determines when the window begins and ends.

---

## 6. Resolving the Loot Roll

Each participating player makes a **straight d20 roll**.

The roll cannot be affected by any factors.

The highest result wins.

If multiple players tie for the highest result:

- Only the tied players reroll.
    
- The reroll is another straight d20.
    
- Rerolls continue until there is one winner.
    

Once a winner has been determined, the result is final and the game continues.

---

## 7. Inspiration and Doom Tokens

Because Inspiration and Doom Tokens are special table-wide mechanics:

- An **Inspiration Token** may still be declared before the loot-roll outcome is revealed, producing an automatic win for that player.
    
- A **Doom Token** may be spent by the DM before the outcome is revealed, causing that player’s loot roll to automatically fail.
    
- If Inspiration and Doom are applied to the same player’s roll, they interact according to the established Inspiration and Doom Token rules.
    

Only one player may receive an automatic win. If multiple Inspiration Tokens are declared for the same item, the affected players make a straight contested reroll against one another.

---

## 8. Final Authority

The DM has final authority over:

- Whether loot is Designated or Contested.
    
- The exact location of the loot.
    
- Which characters are within 30 feet.
    
- Whether an unobstructed route exists.
    
- When the 10-second window begins and ends.
    
- Whether a declaration was made in time.
    
- The resolution of unusual circumstances.
    

These decisions may be discussed after the session but do not delay or interrupt play.

> **Summary:** Designated Loot goes directly to its intended character. All other loot is contested by eligible characters within 30 feet who call their roll within 10 seconds. Highest straight d20 wins.
